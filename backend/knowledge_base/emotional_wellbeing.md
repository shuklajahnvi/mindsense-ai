## Emotional wellbeing is about more than the absence of difficulty

Emotional wellbeing includes being able to notice and handle a range of feelings, feeling connected to others, and having a sense of purpose. All emotions - including sadness, anger, fear and disappointment - are normal parts of human experience. Difficult emotions are not a sign that something is "wrong" with a person; they often carry useful information about what matters to us.

## Five steps to mental wellbeing (NHS)

1. **Connect with other people.** Good relationships help build a sense of belonging and self-worth, and give opportunities to share positive experiences and receive support. This could mean setting aside time with family, arranging a call with a friend, or having lunch with a colleague.
2. **Be physically active.** Activity raises self-esteem and can cause chemical changes in the brain that help positively change mood. It does not need to be intense - walking, cycling or dancing all count.
3. **Learn new skills.** Learning can boost self-confidence and give a sense of purpose. Examples include cooking something new, taking on a new responsibility, or picking up a hobby.
4. **Give to others.** Acts of kindness, large or small - thanking someone, offering help, or volunteering - can create positive feelings and a sense of reward.
5. **Pay attention to the present moment (mindfulness).** Noticing your thoughts, feelings, body and surroundings can help you enjoy life more and understand yourself better.

## Additional self-care ideas (NIMH)

NIMH suggests regular exercise, eating regular healthy meals and staying hydrated, making sleep a priority, trying a relaxing activity, setting goals and priorities (and learning to say "no" to new tasks when overloaded), practising gratitude, focusing on positivity by challenging unhelpful thoughts, and staying connected with people who can provide emotional support.

Self-care is not a cure for mental illness. If you notice severe or distressing symptoms that last two weeks or more - such as difficulty sleeping, changes in appetite, struggling to get out of bed, or loss of interest in activities you usually enjoy - NIMH recommends speaking with a health care provider.
