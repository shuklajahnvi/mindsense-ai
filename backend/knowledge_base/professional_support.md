## When to reach out for professional support

It can be a good idea to speak with a doctor, counsellor, psychologist or other qualified professional if:

- difficult feelings, low mood, worry or stress last for more than a couple of weeks or keep coming back;
- they make it hard to work, study, sleep, eat, or look after yourself;
- you are relying on alcohol, drugs or other risky behaviour to cope;
- people close to you are worried about you;
- you simply want help understanding what you are going through.

You don't need to be "in crisis" to ask for help. A family doctor or GP is often a good first step; many universities and workplaces also offer counselling or employee assistance programmes.

## If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide

If you are in immediate danger, or worried that you might act on thoughts of harming yourself, **contact your local emergency number now** (for example 911 in the US and Canada, 999 or 112 in the UK, 112 in the EU, 112 in India, 000 in Australia) or go to the nearest emergency department.

Free, confidential crisis support:

- **United States:** call or text **988** (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), or chat at 988lifeline.org.
- **Canada:** call or text **988** (Suicide Crisis Helpline).
- **United Kingdom and Republic of Ireland:** call Samaritans on **116 123** (free, 24/7).
- **India:** call Tele-MANAS on **14416** or **1-800-891-4416** (free, 24/7).
- **Australia:** call Lifeline on **13 11 14**.
- **Elsewhere:** findahelpline.com lists free, confidential helplines by country.

## Talking to someone you trust

Sharing what you are going through with a friend, family member, teacher or colleague can be a first step. You might say: "I've been finding things hard lately and I'd like to talk." If someone tells you they are struggling, listening without judgement and encouraging them to seek support can make a real difference.
