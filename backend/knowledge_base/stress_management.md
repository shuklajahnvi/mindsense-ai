## What stress is

Stress is a natural human response to situations that feel challenging or threatening. Everyone experiences some degree of stress, and short bursts of it can help people focus and respond to demands. Stress becomes more of a concern when it is intense or continues for a long time, because ongoing stress can affect sleep, appetite, concentration, mood and physical health.

Common signs people notice include feeling overwhelmed or irritable, racing thoughts, difficulty concentrating, muscle tension, headaches, changes in sleep, and withdrawing from activities or people. Noticing these signs early makes it easier to respond to them.

## Practical ways to respond to stress

- **Notice and name what you are feeling.** Pausing to recognise thoughts and feelings, without judging them, can create a little distance from them. The WHO guide describes this as "noticing" difficult thoughts and feelings and then "unhooking" from them by refocusing on what you are doing.
- **Ground yourself in the present.** Slow breathing, feeling your feet on the floor, or paying attention to what you can see and hear can help when stress feels overwhelming.
- **Act on your values.** Doing small things that matter to you - being kind, helping someone, finishing a small task - can help even when circumstances are hard.
- **Break problems into steps.** Writing down what is within your control and choosing one small next step can make a large problem feel more manageable.
- **Keep a routine.** Regular times for sleep, meals, movement and rest give the day structure.
- **Move your body.** Regular physical activity, including a short walk, is consistently linked with better mood and lower stress.
- **Stay connected.** Talking with friends, family or a trusted person can reduce the sense of carrying things alone.
- **Limit things that add to stress.** Constantly checking news or social media, excess caffeine, and alcohol can all make stress feel worse.
- **Make room for relaxation.** Activities you find calming - music, time outdoors, hobbies, relaxation or mindfulness exercises - are worth scheduling, not only fitting in when there is spare time.

## When to seek more support

NIMH and the NHS advise talking to a health professional if stress is affecting daily life for several weeks, if you are struggling to cope, if you are using alcohol or other substances to cope, or if you have thoughts of harming yourself. If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, contact emergency services or a crisis line immediately.
