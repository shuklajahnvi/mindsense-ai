## Why writing about feelings can help

Research on expressive writing, pioneered by psychologist James Pennebaker and summarised by the American Psychological Association, found that writing about thoughts and feelings around stressful experiences - often for 15 to 20 minutes on a few occasions - was associated with benefits for many participants. Researchers suggest writing may help people organise experiences into a more coherent story and make sense of them. Effects vary between people, and writing is not a substitute for professional care.

Studies on "affect labelling" (Lieberman and colleagues, 2007) found that putting feelings into words was associated with reduced activity in brain regions linked to emotional reactivity. In everyday terms: naming an emotion precisely ("I feel disappointed and a bit embarrassed") rather than vaguely ("I feel bad") may make it easier to work with.

## Reflective journaling prompts

- What happened today that I keep thinking about? What feelings came up?
- What emotion am I noticing most right now? Where do I feel it in my body?
- What was within my control today, and what wasn't?
- What is one thing that went well, however small?
- If a close friend wrote this entry, what would I say to them?
- What do I need right now - rest, connection, movement, help, or something else?
- What is one small step I could take tomorrow?

## Tips for a healthy journaling practice

- Write freely without worrying about spelling or structure.
- Balance writing about difficulties with noticing what helped or went well, so journaling doesn't become only rumination.
- Look for patterns over time - for example, which situations or times of day bring up certain feelings - rather than judging single days.
- Mindfulness - paying attention to thoughts and feelings as they are, without judgement - can complement journaling. The NHS describes it as noticing the present moment, including your thoughts and feelings and the world around you.
- If journaling repeatedly brings up overwhelming distress, it may be a sign to talk with someone you trust or a professional.
